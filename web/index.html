A robot that plans in its imagination

How it works

  1. See. A small CNN encoder turns the 48×40 picture into a 64-number latent state.
  2. Imagine. A predictor guesses the next latent for each of the 4 moves, without touching the real world. Doing this from every cell gives an imagined map of where every move leads.
  3. Plan. It tries all 64 three-move sequences in imagination and scores each by how close it gets to the goal on that imagined map. Every imagined move is snapped to the closest cell picture it knows before the next one, so its imagination can't drift; moves it isn't sure about are treated as walls.
  4. Act. It makes the first move, looks again, and re-plans.

Why JEPA. The encoder is trained together with the predictor, against a slowly updated copy of itself, so that the latent space makes moves predictable rather than just redrawing the picture. On 1,200 maps it never saw (normal maps, dense walls and winding mazes), it reaches the goal by a shortest path 100% of the time. A model with the same networks trained to redraw the next picture instead solves 79% of the mazes.

Trained on 70 maps with 3–7 walls; the goal is always top-right. The model runs entirely in your browser (about 1 MB of weights, no server). Built from the project's PyTorch checkpoints and checked against them number for number.
